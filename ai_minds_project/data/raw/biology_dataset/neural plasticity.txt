Neural plasticity, also known as brain plasticity, refers to the brain’s capacity to reorganize itself by forming new neural connections throughout life. This ability allows the brain to adapt to learning, experience, injury, and environmental changes. When we learn a new skill, practice a task, or recover from brain damage, neural circuits are modified to improve efficiency or compensate for lost functions. Factors such as age, physical activity, mental stimulation, and sleep strongly influence neural plasticity. This phenomenon plays a key role in memory formation, rehabilitation after stroke, and treatment of neurological disorders.
